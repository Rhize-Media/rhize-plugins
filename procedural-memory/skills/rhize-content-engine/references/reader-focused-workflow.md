# Reader-focused article and derivative workflow

Required companion to the canonical Content Engine skill. Apply these steps to new articles,
revisions and article-derived posts. Source verification and output permissions remain separate gates.

## 1. Freeze the reader brief

Before titles or outlines, record:

- Audience and situation: who needs this, what they already know and the task in front of them.
- Reader question: the natural-language query they would ask or search.
- Practical outcome: what they should be able to understand, compare or do after reading.
- Evidence: source identities/dates, supported claims, observed results versus hypothetical examples,
  and limitations that would change the reader's decision.
- Search intent: how-to, comparison, troubleshooting, explanation or decision support. Record actual
  keyword/SERP evidence when available; label inferred queries as hypotheses, with unavailable metrics null.
- Destination and next action: article scope, requested derivatives and permitted draft effects; publishing is out of scope for this workflow.

These are editorial brief fields, not a new registry schema. Use the selected workflow's supported
input fields or a source attachment. Do not fabricate keyword demand or silently modify a signed graph.

## 2. Choose the title before outlining

Propose a small set of titles and choose the one that most clearly answers the reader question.
An internal-name title requires measured keyword/SERP evidence or an explicit user instruction
recorded in the brief. Name the familiar product when it helps discovery; explain the internal plugin or method inside.
A title promises an answer the article must deliver. Do not force every title into a question or
"How to" formula (declarative task titles also qualify), keyword-stuff, or imply availability or results the sources cannot support.

| Internal framing | Reader framing |
|---|---|
| Perplexity Research Bridge | How to Use Perplexity Research in Codex or Claude Code |
| Decision Pre-Mortem Skill | How to Use AI to Stress-Test a Business Decision |
| Our New CRM Integration | How to Connect Manufacturing Quotes to Your CRM |

Examples are framing patterns, not evidence that a particular integration exists. Verify capability
before adopting a title. Match H1, SEO title, description and proposed slug to the same promise.
For existing URLs, preserve redirects, canonical links and dependent posts through normal release review.

## 3. Answer, explain, demonstrate

Open with a concise answer and the relevant situation. Organize sections around the reader's next
questions: prerequisites, steps, decisions, examples and troubleshooting. Use descriptive headings
and self-contained explanations useful to both people and answer engines. Add FAQs only for real
unanswered questions; avoid duplicate answers or guarantees of ranking/citation.

Show the connection between a capability and a concrete task. A supplier comparison can keep
questions and cited evidence together; that does not establish hours saved or better purchasing outcomes.
Label hypothetical scenarios. Include cost, access, platform, maintenance or review constraints where
they affect the choice. Technical details belong where they help the intended reader act.

## 4. Produce clean final copy

Keep the reader's attention on the task. Replace stale or unsupported assertions with verified
information or useful decision guidance. Rewrite the paragraph so it reads naturally as a whole.
Keep the change log and evidence audit in the private editorial packet, not in the public narrative.

| Copy needing revision | Useful final direction |
|---|---|
| The revised publication omits our earlier timing figures. | Measure drafting, review and delivery separately, then compare the same task and acceptance criteria. |
| Our case study does not prove custom software is always cheaper. | Compare the same users, integrations, implementation, support and maintenance scope. |
| We tracked the real numbers instead of guessing. | Describe the supported method or finding; include numbers only when verified. |

Retain a substantive caution such as "Review specifications and the intended application before
approving a substitute part." It helps the reader. Do not strip evidence limits merely to sound
confident. A material public error may need explicit disclosure; raise that editorial decision
rather than silently hiding it. Routine title/copy polishing does not need an apology or correction note.

## 5. Adapt requested derivatives

Use the article's final reader question, claim boundaries and verified destination URL.
If the article is unpublished, mark the link pending and hold link-dependent derivatives.
Never invent a URL from a proposed slug. Choose one
useful point per post. Adapt the opening, detail and call to action for the platform and the author's
voice. A reader should understand the post without knowing the project name or previous version.
Recheck derivatives whenever the article's framing, claim or URL changes. Preserve existing holds;
draft upload does not authorize scheduling, publication or reviewer messages.

## 6. Review the complete package

Save `editorial-review.md` with the private run evidence and attach it to the validation stage.
Record pass or revise/hold, with a short excerpt or reason, for each check:

1. Does the title match a recognizable reader task/query and the answer actually delivered?
2. Does the opening answer it directly rather than announce our project?
3. Can the reader take a useful next step using the examples, steps or comparison?
4. Are benefits and factual claims supported, with material uncertainty near the claim?
5. Is the public copy free of internal correction history and defensive audit narration?
6. Do the metadata and destination links match the final framing?
7. Does each requested derivative stand alone and preserve the same evidence limits?
8. Is the material-correction decision recorded (needed/not needed), with a reason?

Revise failed copy and repeat the relevant checks before delivery. Hold unresolved factual claims.
Any open review hold prevents completed status. If a correction needs an unauthorized CMS write,
finish partial with this evidence and do not deliver the graph output as a finished article.
The review record is editorial evidence, not a deterministic proof of quality. Keep the original,
final version and Humanizer diff privately; preserve ordinary source and benchmark capture.
